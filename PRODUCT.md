# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML generated by a dependency-free Python build script from per-lesson data files, deployed to GitHub Pages from `main` `/docs`.

Not an open decision. Both sibling sites (`year8-music`, `year10-music-2026`) are built this way, for a reason recorded in the Year 10 record and still binding: the teacher writes no code and will not be in a session when something breaks, so a framework's dependency tree is a liability rather than a convenience. No lockfile, no install step, nothing to rot.

Repo name `year11-music-2027`, publishing at `https://edwards-resources.github.io/year11-music-2027/`. **The repo name is load-bearing**: the base path is compiled into every internal link, so renaming the repo breaks the whole site and its Pages URL at once.

## Users

**Primary: the 2027 Year 11 Music 1 class**, aged about 16 to 17, senior elective students. They work from the site in class on school devices during 60-minute periods, and at home when catching up, revising a work they need to know for the examination, or writing their composition log. Unlike the junior cohorts they are studying toward a formal examination, so they return to the same material repeatedly rather than reading a lesson once and moving on.

**This class is the 2026 Year 10 cohort**, who spent 2026 inside the Year 10 site's Marker Zine world. They arrive already fluent in how these sites work and expecting the senior course to look like a step up.

**Secondary: the teacher, teaching from it live.** He projects the page and works down it with the class, in a daylit classroom, and does not want to click in and out of Canvas modules mid-lesson to reach a recording.

## Product Purpose

A live teaching site carrying the Year 11 Music 1 lesson content for Terms 1 to 3 of 2027: what each lesson covers, what to listen to, what to do, and what the class is expected to be able to do by the end of it. It replaces Canvas content pages entirely.

Success is a full lesson taught from the site without opening anything else, and a student revising for the AT3 aural examination finding every work they studied without asking where it was.

## Positioning

**The works are the spine, not the lessons.**

The junior sites are lesson-shaped: a lesson happens, it is done, the class moves on. A senior music course does not work that way. Twenty-two works are studied across the year, they recur across terms, and the end-of-year aural examination draws its excerpts from that list. A student revising in Term 3 needs to get back to a work met in Term 1 Week 2 without knowing which lesson it was in.

No other tool in the stack holds that. Canvas holds assessment; the registered program holds the sequence; only this site can present the year as a body of repertoire a student has accumulated, with the class's real position marked inside it.

## Operating Context

- NSW public school, NESA **Music 1 11-12 Syllabus (2025)**, first taught to Year 11 in 2027. Three terms only: Terms 1 to 3 of 2027, since Term 4 2027 is Year 12 work.
- Three focus areas, one per term: **Contexts of music**, **Creative practice**, **Music in focus (music for film and screen)**.
- 10-week terms, fortnightly A/B timetable cycle, 60-minute periods.
- Lessons slip constantly through cover, carnivals, assemblies and examination blocks. The gap between the registered sequence and the real position is the normal state.
- Daily debriefs are the update ritual: content and position changes flow from the debrief into the repo, then to the site.
- **Canvas holds assessment and only assessment**: the three task notifications, submission points, rubrics and SpeedGrader, and the course documents. Decided 11 August 2026 and now the standing rule for every course: no teaching content on Canvas at all. The site links out to Canvas; it never duplicates it.
- The registered documents (scope and sequence v2.1, three unit programs, the assessment bundle) are the authority. The site presents them and must never contradict them.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Must carry:** the full registered lesson sequence for all three terms (about 50 lesson pages); per-lesson learning intentions, key-concept tables, worked examples, in-class activities and success criteria; the outcome codes each lesson works toward; the year's repertoire as a first-class, addressable thing; embedded listening; and a visible marker of where the class is up to.

**Live position: yes**, on the Year 10 model, decided 11 August 2026. The site carries both the registered sequence and the class's real position in it, updated from debriefs.

**Music 1 only. No Music Life Skills track**, decided 11 August 2026. The Life Skills parallel exists in the unit programs and the assessment bundle, and Canvas carries the Life Skills tasks; no Life Skills lesson content exists on disk and none is to be invented for the site. Adjustment happens live in the room.

**The five Term 3 practice papers do not go on the site.** They are real HSC questions with NESA's audio and NESA's marking guidelines. Those weeks get a page carrying what the paper covers, the command words, how it is marked and the 2009-to-2025 terminology bridge, and nothing else. The paper is handed out in class and lives in Canvas. Decided 11 August 2026.

**Must never carry:** student names, student work, marks, Sentral or markbook data, NESA past papers or marking guidelines, commercial audio or video files, or the internal repertoire register's private content (the cohort-exclusion list and the held-back reasoning are working documents, not student-facing). Audio and video are embedded from their host, never uploaded.

**Hosting:** GitHub Pages, public repository, publicly reachable with no login, `noindex` on every page. Repos live in the free `Edwards-Resources` GitHub organisation, keeping teaching repos separate from personal app repos.

**School identity: none.** No school logo, letterhead or brand assets, and no school name anywhere in the repository, including in internal planning files. The site is anonymous by decision, and its identity is its own.

**Unresolved and not to be assumed either way:** whether NSW Department of Education policy places conditions on staff publishing teaching material to a personal external host. Carried from the sibling sites; the teacher is content to proceed and it is not to be re-raised as a blocker.

## Brand Commitments

- Australian English throughout.
- No em dashes in anything student-facing or teacher-facing.
- **A visual world of its own, and a deliberate step up in seniority.** Each year group gets its own world and the others are anti-references. Year 10's Marker Zine and Year 8's Tour Tee are both confirmed anti-references here, and the `sample/` Rehearsal Marks world rejected during the Year 8 build is a third. The reason is sharper for this site than for the others: this cohort is coming straight off the Marker Zine, so a hand-drawn or merch-print register would read as a sideways move into a senior elective.
- **World: On Air**, chosen 11 August 2026. The year as a music station's schedule, and the class is what is on air right now: each term a strand, the lessons its slots, the repertoire its archive. Binding. It replaced **The Liner Notes** (the year as a record collection), which was chosen earlier the same day against The Desk and The Season, built as three comps, and then rejected outright by Matthew as too shouty, too dark and heavy, and hard to use. The Liner Notes is an anti-reference now, alongside Year 10's Marker Zine and Year 8's Tour Tee; its comps are kept in `.impeccable/mocks/superseded/` and are not to be mined for parts. See `DIRECTION.md` for the contract this world established.
- **Distinctive and engaging, not a plain school course site**, carried forward from the sibling sites' explicit direction. The structural virtues still hold inside the world: unambiguous course to term to lesson hierarchy, visible position, short scannable lesson pages, generous type, accessibility as a requirement.

## Product Principles

1. **The registered program is the spine; the live position is the overlay.** Never let the second quietly rewrite the first, because the program is a compliance document.
2. **A work is a destination, not a footnote.** If a student can reach a lesson but not a work, the site has failed the course it serves.
3. **A lesson page is used with hands busy**, holding a pen and listening. Anything needing careful reading to operate has failed.
4. **Public means public.** Every addition is judged on whether it would be safe on a billboard, because functionally it is on one.
5. **It has to survive the teacher not being in a session.** No dependency, build step or service that breaks unattended.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The site must remain fully usable with browser zoom and text scaling, meet WCAG AA contrast, and never depend on colour alone to convey position or lesson status. It has to hold on a projector in a daylit classroom and on a phone at home, which is a contrast and type-scaling requirement before it is an aesthetic one. Listening activities must not assume headphones or a particular device.
