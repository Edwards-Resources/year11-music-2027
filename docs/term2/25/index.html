Theme and variations: a worked case in Western art music

Now listening
Young Person’s Guide to the Orchestra
Britten  ·  M 10

Britten’s theme is Purcell’s; each variation reassigns it to a different instrument family, the clearest available case of one element tracked through change.

By the end you can

Track one element of a theme through its variations, and describe for at least two variations what changed and what stayed constant.

Outcomes
MU1-11L-01
MU1-11L-02

Listen

Britten’s Young Person’s Guide to the Orchestra: the theme is Purcell’s; each variation reassigns it to a different section.

Why this matters

This extends the Term 1 Western art music repertoire category. A theme and variations makes the logic of variation explicit and countable: the same theme is presented, then reworked a fixed number of times, which is why it is a useful worked case before applying the same thinking to freer forms.

In class this fortnight

  • 01Listen to the theme alone first, without any variations, and note its key features (melody, harmony, rhythm, texture).
  • 02Listen to each variation in turn, tracking one chosen element (for example melody, or texture) across all of them.
  • 03For each variation, note what changed and what was held constant against the theme.
  • 04Well-formed dot points summarising the overall shape of the set: does it escalate in complexity, or move in some other pattern?
