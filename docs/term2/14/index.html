Lesson 14: Improvisation as a way of generating material

Lesson 14 of 50 · Creating and composing

Learning intention

Use improvisation to generate raw material for a composition, not to perform a finished idea. Record every attempt, including the ones that get rejected, and select material by listening back rather than judging while playing.

By the end you can: Record multiple improvised takes over a fixed frame, and explain in the log why one fragment was kept over the others.

Outcomes MU1-11C-01

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Set a fixed harmonic or rhythmic frame (a two-chord loop, or a repeating rhythmic groove) for the class or small groups to improvise over.
  2. Record every attempt, four to six takes minimum, without stopping to judge mid-take.
  3. Listen back to all takes and select the strongest fragment, in writing, before moving on.
  4. Log entry: the frame used, every take kept and rejected, and why the selected fragment was chosen.

Listening

Teacher’s choice

Confirmed in class.

LearnWhy this matters

Composers who wait for the finished idea to arrive in their head rarely start. Improvising over a fixed frame turns composing into a physical, repeatable habit: play, listen back, keep or discard, play again. This builds directly on the Term 1 improvisation work and is the generating stage the AT2 composition log documents from the start.

LearnWhat counts as evidence

The takes a student rejects are as much evidence of process as the take they keep. A log that only shows the final choice looks like it arrived by luck; a log that shows what was tried and why it was dropped shows judgement, which is what the composition log criteria reward.
