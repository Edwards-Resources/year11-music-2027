Model works: how a musical idea is built

Now listening
Teacher’s choice

Confirmed in class.

By the end you can

Point to a bar in a work where an idea is developed rather than repeated, and say what specifically changed.

Outcomes
MU1-11C-01

Three things that can happen to an idea

MoveWhat it meansWhat to listen for
RepetitionThe idea returns unchanged, or close to it.Does the ear recognise it instantly, with nothing new to notice?
VariationThe idea returns changed in one dimension: rhythm, register, instrumentation, harmony.What single thing is different? What stayed the same?
DevelopmentThe idea is broken apart, extended, combined with other material, or taken somewhere new.Can you still point to the fragment of the original idea inside it?

Why this matters

A short idea is not a piece. What turns four bars into a three-minute work is what a composer does to that idea afterwards. Composers plan for development from the start, which is why AT2 asks for a composition log rather than a finished file with no history.

Worked example

Take a two-bar rhythmic motif on a single pitch. Repeated, it is a riff. Moved up a third with the same rhythm, it is a variation. Split into two one-bar fragments passed between instruments, with the gap between them filled by new material, it is development. The listener can still hear the original motif inside the development, which is what makes it development rather than a new idea replacing the old one.

In class this fortnight

  • 01Diagnostic listening: two contrasting works, no labels yet. Note where the opening idea returns, and where the music feels different from the opening without being unrelated to it.
  • 02Track the idea by ear first, then check against a score or DAW view of the same passage.
  • 03Mark on paper the exact bar where repetition stops and development starts, and justify the choice in one sentence.
  • 04Log entry: name one development technique heard today that could be used on the student’s own AT2 idea.
