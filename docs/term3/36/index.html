Notation systems used in screen music

SystemWhat it shows
Cue sheetTiming, instrumentation and cue titles against the film’s timecode
Lead sheetMelody and chord symbols, for a quick read of the harmonic and melodic shape
Full score reductionA condensed version of the orchestral score, readable at a glance
Click and tempo mapThe exact tempo, and any tempo changes, locked to the film’s timing
DAW representationsPiano roll (pitch and duration), beat grid (rhythmic timing) and waveform (amplitude over time)

Why this matters

Each notation system shows something the others hide. A cue sheet shows timing but not pitch; a lead sheet shows harmony but not exact orchestration; a click map shows tempo but nothing musical at all. Reading screen music fluently means knowing which system to reach for.

In class this fortnight

  • 01Examine examples of each notation system in the concept table.
  • 02Follow a single cue in two of these systems at once (for example a lead sheet alongside a DAW piano roll), noting what each shows that the other does not.
  • 03Well-formed dot points: for the cue followed, one thing the first system showed and the second did not, and vice versa.
