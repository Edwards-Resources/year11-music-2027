Leitmotif and thematic transformation

Listen

Howard Shore’s Concerning Hobbits, from The Fellowship of the Ring: leitmotif and thematic transformation.

Why this matters

A theme carries story by being transformed, not repeated. Orchestral screen scoring meets the mandatory Western art music repertoire category here, in a screen context, so this topic serves both the focus area and that requirement.

Worked example

A character’s theme first appears in a major key on solo horn, warm and open. Later, after that character is betrayed, the same melodic contour returns in a minor key, on strings, slower, with a dissonant accompaniment. The notes are recognisably the same shape; what changed (mode, instrumentation, tempo, harmony) tells the audience how the character or their situation has changed.

In class this fortnight

  • 01Track one theme across a whole film, or a long excerpt, noting every appearance.
  • 02For each transformation, describe what changed, what stayed the same, and what the change tells the audience.
  • 03Compare two appearances of the same theme and judge, with evidence, which one better serves its scene.
  • 04Well-formed dot points recording the strongest transformation found and the judgement made about it.
