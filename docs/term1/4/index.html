Lesson 4: Harmony by ear: chords I, IV, V and vi

Lesson 4 of 50 · Theoretical language

Learning intention

Identify chords I, IV, V and vi by ear in songs, play simple progressions on keyboard or guitar, and explain how the chords support the melody and mood.

By the end you can: Find the tonic, hear a move to IV, V or vi, and play a I–V–vi–IV progression.

Outcomes MU1-11L-02

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Find the tonic (I). Hum the note that feels like home.
  2. When the chord changes, ask: does it feel like it is moving away (IV), pulling back home (V), or going darker (vi)?
  3. Play the bass note: the lowest note often names the chord.
  4. Test your guess by playing the progression on a keyboard, then identify it in a teacher-chosen song by ear.

Listening

Teacher’s choice

No work from the register is pinned to this lesson; a teacher-chosen song carries the identification activity in class.

Where this fits

Chords ii and V7 belong to the next focus area (Creative practice). Stay with I, IV, V and vi for now.
