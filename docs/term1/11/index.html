Lesson 11: Unit review and vocabulary check

Lesson 11 of 50 · Listening, analysing and responding

Learning intention

Pull the whole term together. Revisit Music context and Music language, check the term’s vocabulary against your own glossary, and put those words into a well-formed dot point, which is the writing shape the rest of this course is built on.

By the end you can: Use this term’s vocabulary correctly in the cumulative review quiz, and write a well-formed dot point about a work you have studied: the feature named, the evidence you heard, and the effect it has.

Outcomes MU1-11L-01 MU1-11L-02

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Cumulative review quiz, twelve questions across the whole term: Music context and Music language, rhythmic and melodic devices, tonality and harmony by ear, metre, notation types and production techniques.
  2. Glossary audit. Work back through the unit glossary you have been building since Week 1. Any term you cannot define in your own words gets rewritten, in your own words, before Term 2.
  3. Listening recall: short excerpts from works already studied, named cold. For each, give the style, one convention you can hear, and one Music language feature.
  4. Write one well-formed dot point on a work from this term, then swap and check a partner’s against the same three parts.
  5. Name one thing from Contexts of music you expect to carry into Creative practice next term.

Worked exampleA well-formed dot point

A well-formed dot point has three parts and fits on one line: the feature you are talking about, the evidence you actually heard, and the effect it has. One element per dot point, and never a paragraph. Take Bill Withers' Ain’t No Sunshine from Lesson 2. A weak answer says the song is sad, which names no feature and gives no evidence. A well-formed one says: the vocal line is built on one short repeated idea, and in the middle section the lyric drops away to a single phrase repeated over the same accompaniment, which holds the listener on one thought instead of moving the story on. Feature, evidence, effect. This is the shape every listening answer takes from here to the end of Year 12, so it is worth getting automatic now rather than in the week before an examination.

The review quiz

Twelve questions, one mark each, covering the whole of Contexts of music. It is a check on where your vocabulary actually is, not an assessment task: Assessment Task 1 was Week 8 and nothing here is weighted. Use the result to decide what goes back into your glossary over the break.
