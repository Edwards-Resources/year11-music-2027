Lesson 1: Style, genre and conventions

Lesson 1 of 50 · Listening, analysing and responding

Learning intention

Define style, genre and musical conventions, and tell them apart. Name conventions you can hear in a work and say what era, place or culture they point to.

By the end you can: Name the style and genre of a work, point to two conventions you can hear, and explain one way its context shapes the sound, using correct terminology.

Outcomes MU1-11L-01

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Diagnostic listening: jot what you notice about a short excerpt, with no labels yet.
  2. Compare contrasting works, for example a 12-bar blues, an Australian pop song, and a work from a non-Western tradition chosen with cultural protocols.
  3. For each, name the style and genre, then list two conventions you can hear.
  4. Add the new terms to your unit glossary.

CompareThe three ideas inside "Music context"

TermPlain meaningA question to ask of a work
StyleThe characteristic manner in which music is composed, performed or understood.What makes this sound like the thing it is?
GenreA category of music based on formal and cultural criteria.What family of music does this belong to?
Musical conventionsThe established practices, norms and rules that govern a genre, culture or period.What 'rules' is this music following?

Worked exampleHearing conventions

Take a 12-bar blues. The conventions you can point to include a repeating 12-bar chord pattern built on chords I, IV and V, a swung rhythmic feel, blue notes in the melody, and a call-and-response shape between voice and instrument. Those conventions are what make it recognisable as blues rather than, say, a march or a pop ballad.
