Style, genre and conventions

Now listening
Eine kleine Nachtmusik
Mozart  ·  M 01

Also meet M 02 Oye Como Va, M 03 Libertango and M 04 Chan Chan across these two weeks: one frame held against three different global traditions.

By the end you can

Name the style and genre of a work, point to two conventions you can hear, and explain one way its context shapes the sound, using correct terminology.

The three ideas inside "Music context"

TermPlain meaningA question to ask of a work
StyleThe characteristic manner in which music is composed, performed or understood.What makes this sound like the thing it is?
GenreA category of music based on formal and cultural criteria.What family of music does this belong to?
Musical conventionsThe established practices, norms and rules that govern a genre, culture or period.What 'rules' is this music following?

Worked example: hearing conventions

Take a 12-bar blues. The conventions you can point to include a repeating 12-bar chord pattern built on chords I, IV and V, a swung rhythmic feel, blue notes in the melody, and a call-and-response shape between voice and instrument. Those conventions are what make it recognisable as blues rather than, say, a march or a pop ballad.

In class this fortnight

  • 01Diagnostic listening: jot what you notice about a short excerpt, with no labels yet.
  • 02Compare contrasting works, for example a 12-bar blues, an Australian pop song, and a work from a non-Western tradition chosen with cultural protocols.
  • 03For each, name the style and genre, then list two conventions you can hear.
  • 04Add the new terms to your unit glossary.

Success criteria

ICIP protocol

Where any activity uses Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander music or cultural material, follow NESA ICIP protocols. Work in partnership with Elders, Community members, Cultural Knowledge Holders or your local AECG, and do not present sacred or restricted material.
