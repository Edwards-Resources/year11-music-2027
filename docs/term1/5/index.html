Lesson 5: Motifs and development in the DAW

Lesson 5 of 50 · Creating and composing

Learning intention

Create a short motif using Compositional language, develop it with rhythmic, melodic and harmonic devices, and document each stage of your idea in the DAW.

By the end you can: Create a short motif, develop it with at least one named device, and document the change in your DAW.

Outcomes MU1-11C-01 MU1-11C-02

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Week 2: improvise and capture a short motif that reflects a chosen tradition.
  2. Week 4: extend the motif using rhythmic and melodic devices.
  3. Week 8: add a simple I, IV, V, vi progression under the motif.
  4. Week 10: get feedback, refine, and export an audio file.

Listening

Teacher’s choice

A creating lesson, not a listening one. GarageBand on the iPads or Flat.io for notation.

CompareWays to develop a motif

ApproachWhat to try
RhythmicAdd syncopation, turn it into an ostinato, or augment the note values.
MelodicMove it up or down as a sequence, invert it, or turn it into a riff.
TexturalAdd a countermelody, double it an octave up, or loop it.
HarmonicPlace it over I, IV, V or vi and hear how the mood changes.

LearnDocumenting is assessable practice

For each sketch, save a new version and write one or two lines: what the idea is, what device you used, and what you will try next. This habit feeds directly into the Year 11 composition development requirements.

Using First Nations material in your work

If you want to build on Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander musical material in a sketch, talk to your teacher before you start. Some material is sacred or restricted and is not yours to sample, imitate or rework.
