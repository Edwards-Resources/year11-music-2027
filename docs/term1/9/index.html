Lesson 9: Notation in context: lead sheets, tab, drum and staff

Lesson 9 of 50 · Notational language

Learning intention

Read and interpret lead sheets, tablature, drum notation and staff notation, match the notation type to the repertoire and tradition, and transcribe a short idea into an appropriate notation.

By the end you can: Read each notation type, say where it is used, and transcribe a short idea into a suitable form.

Outcomes MU1-11L-02 MU1-11L-01

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Choose a short riff from your repertoire.
  2. Write it in two forms: staff notation and one other (tab or drum notation).
  3. Swap with a partner and play each other’s transcriptions.

Listening

Teacher’s choice

Listening and transcription happen in class, from a short riff in your own repertoire.

LearnReading a lead sheet

Read the chord symbols above the stave (for example C, F, G, Am) as you follow the melody. The symbols tell you the harmony; the notes give the tune; the lyrics align underneath.
