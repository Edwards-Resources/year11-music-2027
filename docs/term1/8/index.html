Lesson 8: Technologies and the development of styles

Lesson 8 of 50 · Listening, analysing and responding

Learning intention

Explain how recording and production technologies shaped a style, compare contrasting eras of the same style, and use production terms accurately.

By the end you can: Name a technology, point to where you hear its effect, and explain how it shaped the style.

Outcomes MU1-11L-01

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

In classThis fortnight

  1. Listen to an early, lightly produced recording in a style.
  2. Listen to a later, heavily produced recording in the same style.
  3. List three differences you can hear and link each to a technology.
  4. Explain how the technology changed the listener experience.

Listening

Teacher’s choice

Listening examples chosen in class: an early, lightly produced recording against a later, heavily produced one in the same style.

LearnTechnology is part of the story of a style

Styles do not develop in a vacuum. The tools available to record, produce and distribute music have shaped how it sounds. Multitrack recording, electric instruments, sampling and the DAW each changed what was possible.

CompareTechnologies and what they allowed

TechnologyWhat it allowedHeard in
Multitrack recordingLayering parts recorded separately.Studio pop and rock from the 1960s on.
Electric and electronic instrumentsNew timbres and volume.Rock, funk, synth-pop.
SamplingReusing recorded sound as material.Hip hop, dance, electronic music.
The DAWComposing, editing and producing on a computer.Most music made today.

LearnProduction terms to use

Looping, sampling, delay, echo, pitch shifting, digital reverb, electroacoustic, waveform, piano roll, beat grid.
